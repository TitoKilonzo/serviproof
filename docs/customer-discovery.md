# Customer discovery and first-revenue plan

## Target customer

Start with independently operated commercial pest-control companies in Nairobi and nearby counties that:
- employ roughly 3–30 field technicians;
- service offices, restaurants, hotels, warehouses, schools or residential estates;
- have repeat service contracts rather than only one-off residential callouts;
- currently coordinate visits in WhatsApp, spreadsheets, paper job cards or a basic accounting tool; and
- need to share treatment evidence or reports with a facilities/procurement contact.

Avoid selling to a broad list of all service companies in the first month. The point of a vertical wedge is to learn one workflow deeply.

## Interview plan

Speak to 15 operators: 8 owners/general managers, 4 dispatch/operations managers and 3 technicians. Ask for examples of the last completed service visit rather than asking whether they like the idea.

1. Walk me through a typical recurring customer visit, from scheduling through report delivery and payment.
2. How do you know which contracts are due in the next 7, 14 and 30 days?
3. What happens when a technician is sick, late, offline or assigned to the wrong site?
4. How do you prove that the technician attended the correct site and completed the agreed treatment?
5. What information do customers ask for after a service visit? Can you show me a recent report with confidential details hidden?
6. How long does it take to prepare and send that report?
7. What happens when a report, signature or photo is missing during a customer complaint or audit?
8. How do you connect completed work to an invoice and record M-Pesa or bank payments?
9. Which existing software do you pay for today? What does it not solve?
10. Who approves a new software purchase, what budget can they use, and how long does approval take?
11. Would you pay for a 30-day pilot with a real dispatcher and two technicians? What would the pilot need to prove?

Do not pitch the product until the operator has described the current workflow. Record frequency, time spent, mistakes, cost of mistakes and who owns the budget.

## Pilot offer to test

Offer a paid 30-day pilot to 3 operators. Suggested test pricing:

- Starter: KES 2,500/month, single workspace and small team.
- Growth: KES 5,500/month, recurring schedules, field evidence and reporting.
- Business: KES 10,000/month, larger teams and multi-site operations.

These are hypotheses, not market-validated prices. Test monthly versus annual billing and whether each operator prefers pricing by technician, active customer or completed visit.

Include onboarding for the first 30 customers/sites. Do not offer unlimited customisation. Ask each pilot to nominate one owner and one dispatcher who attend a 30-minute weekly review.

## Pilot success metrics

Compare the four weeks before and during the pilot:

- percentage of due visits scheduled before their service date;
- percentage of completed visits with evidence uploaded within 24 hours;
- median time from job completion to report sent;
- missed or unassigned visits per month;
- invoices issued within two business days of completion;
- time spent each week preparing management reports; and
- weekly active use by owner, dispatcher and technician.

A reasonable early signal is 3 operators who use the product weekly, 2 who agree to continue paying, and a measurable reduction in report turnaround or missed visits. Do not interpret friendly feedback as purchase intent.

## 30-day execution sequence

| Days | Work | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | Interview 8 operators; observe one real service workflow | Confirm the top two painful operational delays |
| 6–10 | Interview 7 more; prototype the job-to-proof-to-invoice flow | Three businesses agree to a pilot date |
| 11–17 | Configure customer data, sites, contracts and team users for pilot one | One operator can schedule and assign jobs independently |
| 18–24 | Run actual visits with photos, notes and customer reports | At least 80% of completed pilot visits have complete evidence |
| 25–30 | Review baseline vs pilot outcomes and request paid continuation | At least one paid renewal or a concrete reason to pivot |

## Sales motion

1. Build a named list of 40 pest-control providers from public directories, referrals and local industry networks.
2. Request a short operational walkthrough, not a generic software demo.
3. Demonstrate the workflow using the operator's own customer/site labels and a sample job card.
4. Offer a time-boxed paid pilot with one measurable outcome.
5. Onboard the dispatcher and a technician together; do not only train the owner.
6. Ask for a reference only after the operator has a measured result.

## Reasons to pivot or narrow

- If operators mainly need inventory/chemical compliance rather than recurring work dispatch, narrow the product around treatment records and evidence.
- If they already have dispatch software but customers constantly ask for proof, focus on customer-facing service reports and evidence retention.
- If owner budgets are too small but large facilities firms pay, move upmarket to multi-site service contractors.
- If usage stops after the initial setup, the workflow is not embedded enough. Prioritise daily technician and dispatcher use over adding more dashboard charts.
